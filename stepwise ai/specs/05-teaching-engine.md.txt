Build the StepWise AI Teaching Engine.The Teaching Engine is responsible for turning the AI Brain's understanding of the student's state into an adaptive, interactive teaching experience.The AI Brain understands the student.The Teaching Engine teaches the student.The Teaching Engine must continuously decide:- What should the student do next?- Should the AI intervene?- Should it ask a question?- Should it provide a hint?- Should it show a visual?- Should it simplify the concept?- Should it revisit a prerequisite?- Should it let the student continue independently?- Should it ask the student to correct something?- Has the student demonstrated enough understanding to move forward?The Teaching Engine must never behave like a static answer key.==================================================1. CORE TEACHING PRINCIPLE==================================================The default teaching philosophy is:DO NOT TAKE THE THINKING AWAY FROM THE STUDENT.The AI should provide the smallest useful amount of assistance that allows the student to make progress.Preferred sequence:OBSERVE→ ASK→ HINT→ CLARIFY→ VISUALIZE→ DEMONSTRATE PARTIALLY→ EXPLAIN FULLYDo not automatically jump to the final explanation.==================================================2. TEACHING LOOP==================================================Every meaningful student action should follow this conceptual loop:STUDENT ACTS↓AI OBSERVES↓AI UNDERSTANDS↓AI EVALUATES↓AI DECIDES INTERVENTION LEVEL↓AI RESPONDS↓STUDENT TRIES AGAIN / CONTINUES↓AI RE-EVALUATESThis loop continues until the learning objective is sufficiently demonstrated.==================================================3. INITIAL TEACHING SEQUENCE==================================================When a question is submitted:1. Analyze question.2. Create Learning Blueprint.3. Identify first useful concept.4. Open Board.5. Introduce topic.6. Give first thinking prompt.7. Give optional hint.8. Wait for student action.Example:QUESTION:"What is photosynthesis?"BOARD:🌱 LET'S EXPLORE PHOTOSYNTHESISWe'll build the answer together.FIRST:What do you think photosynthesis means?💡 Hint:Think about what plants do with sunlight.[Start Writing] [🎤 Speak]Do not display the complete answer at this stage.==================================================4. STEP-BASED TEACHING==================================================The AI may divide a complex question into conceptual stages.Example:Photosynthesis:Stage 1:What is it?Stage 2:What does the plant need?Stage 3:Where do those materials come from?Stage 4:What happens during the process?Stage 5:What is produced?Stage 6:Can you explain the entire process?These stages are internal learning checkpoints.Do not make the student feel like they are completing a rigid form.==================================================5. DYNAMIC STEPS==================================================Steps must adapt to the student.If the student already knows Stage 2:Skip or compress it.If the student struggles:Expand it.If the student demonstrates a prerequisite gap:Temporarily move backward.If the student demonstrates mastery:Move toward application or deeper understanding.The teaching path should be generated dynamically.==================================================6. STEP INTRODUCTIONBefore each meaningful new concept, introduce what the student is about to think about.Example:### Step 2 — What does the plant need?"You already identified sunlight as important.Now let's think about the other things the plant needs."Then ask:"What comes from the air?"This creates continuity between steps.==================================================7. DO NOT REVEAL THE WHOLE ROADMAPThe AI may internally know that there are six concepts.The student does not necessarily need to see:1. Definition2. Inputs3. Process4. Products5. Equation6. ApplicationInstead, guide naturally:"Great. Now let's look at what enters the plant."This makes the experience feel like a teacher guiding the student rather than a form being completed.==================================================8. CHECKPOINTSUse conceptual checkpoints.A checkpoint is reached when the student has provided enough evidence about a concept.Example:Checkpoint:"Student understands that sunlight provides energy."The AI may continue.But before considering the whole topic complete, it may later ask:"Can you explain why sunlight is important?"This confirms genuine understanding.==================================================9. HINT SYSTEMHints must be contextual.Never use generic hints like:"Think carefully."or:"Try again."Instead identify the exact conceptual obstacle.Example:Student:"Plants get food from soil."AI:💡 Hint:Think about the difference between the materials a plant absorbs from the soil and the food it produces itself.[Try Again]==================================================10. HINT LEVELSImplement progressive hint levels.### HINT 1 — GentleAsk a broad question.### HINT 2 — DirectionPoint toward the relevant concept.### HINT 3 — SpecificNarrow the possible reasoning.### HINT 4 — ExampleGive a simple related example.### HINT 5 — Partial StructureProvide part of the reasoning.### HINT 6 — Near-SolutionGive enough support that the student can finish.### HINT 7 — ExplanationExplain the concept fully if necessary.Track the number and level of hints used.==================================================11. HINT ADAPTATIONDo not always escalate hints after a fixed number of attempts.Consider:- Student's previous attempts- Error type- Confidence- Age/level- Topic difficulty- Time spent- Whether the student is making progressA student who is close to understanding may need only one small clue.A student missing a prerequisite may need a completely different explanation.==================================================12. STUDENT CAN REQUEST HINTSProvide:💡 HINTThe student can request help whenever they want.When requested, determine the smallest useful hint.Also provide optional:**Stronger Hint**if the first hint isn't enough.==================================================13. STUDENT CAN ASK "WHY?"Every major AI intervention should allow:**Why?**If selected, explain the reasoning behind the feedback.Example:🔴 Concept Error"Plants eat sunlight."Why?> Plants don't consume sunlight as food. Light provides energy that drives photosynthesis.Then return control to the student.==================================================14. ERROR INTERVENTIONWhen a major error is detected:Pause progression.Highlight the relevant content.Attach a label.Explain the issue briefly.Provide a targeted hint.Ask the student to try again.Example:Student:"Plants get glucose from the soil."AI:🔴 CONCEPT ERROR**Let's pause here.**The soil provides water and minerals, but glucose is produced by the plant during photosynthesis.💡 Think:Where does the carbon in glucose come from?[Try Again]Do not immediately give the full answer.==================================================15. PARTIAL ERRORIf the student's response contains correct and incorrect information:Do not mark the whole response wrong.Use:🟡 PARTIALLY CORRECTExample:Student:"Photosynthesis is when plants use sunlight to make food from water."AI:🟡 PARTIALLY CORRECT"You've got an important part right: sunlight and water are involved.There's one important ingredient missing.💡 What gas does the plant take in from the air?"==================================================16. MISSING INFORMATIONIf the student's answer is correct but incomplete:Use:🔵 MISSING IDEAExample:Student:"Plants use sunlight to make glucose."AI:🟢 CORRECT CORE IDEABut your explanation is missing one important part.💡 What happens to oxygen during the process?This encourages expansion rather than rejection.==================================================17. CORRECT ANSWERSDo not immediately end the lesson after a correct response.Determine whether the answer demonstrates:- Recall- Understanding- Application- Deeper reasoningIf sufficient:🟢 CORRECT UNDERSTANDING"Exactly. You identified the main idea."Then continue to the next concept.If the topic requires deeper understanding:"Great. Now let's see if you can use that idea."==================================================18. SELF-CORRECTIONSelf-correction is especially valuable.Example:Student:"Plants get food from soil."Student pauses and changes it to:"Plants make glucose themselves."The AI should recognize the correction.Possible response:🟢 NICE CORRECTION"You caught the key idea yourself."Record:Self-correction = trueThis should positively influence learning analytics.==================================================19. DO NOT OVERPRAISEEncouragement should be genuine.Good:"That correction fixed the main misconception.""Your reasoning is getting more precise.""You connected those two ideas correctly."Avoid constant:"AMAZING!!!""YOU'RE A GENIUS!!!"The tone should feel like an excellent teacher.==================================================20. TEACHING THROUGH QUESTIONSPrefer questions that make the student reason.Instead of:"Carbon dioxide enters through stomata."Ask:"Plants need carbon dioxide. How do you think it gets into the leaf?"If the student struggles:"Look at the tiny openings on the leaf."Then visualize them.==================================================21. SOCRATIC TEACHINGUse guided questions when appropriate.Example:AI:"What happens to the ball if you push it?"Student:"It moves."AI:"What if you push it harder?"Student:"It moves faster."AI:"Good. So what might changing force do to acceleration?"The AI should build understanding through connected questions.Do not use Socratic questioning mechanically for every topic.==================================================22. EXPLANATION SWITCHINGIf one explanation doesn't work, change strategy.For example:Attempt 1:Formal explanation.Student:"I don't understand."Attempt 2:Simple language.Still confused.Attempt 3:Real-world analogy.Still confused.Attempt 4:Diagram.Still confused.Attempt 5:Interactive mini-example.The Teaching Engine should adapt instead of repeating the same explanation.==================================================23. TEACHING METHOD SELECTIONPossible teaching methods:- Direct question- Hint- Analogy- Example- Counterexample- Diagram- Animation- Comparison- Story- Demonstration- Simulation- Formula- Step-by-step reasoning- Real-world application- Mini quiz- Recall prompt- Explain-back activityChoose based on the student's current difficulty.==================================================24. VISUAL INTERVENTIONUse visuals when they make the concept easier.Example:Student struggles to understand blood flow.Instead of another paragraph:Generate a heart diagram.Highlight:RIGHT SIDE→ lungs→ LEFT SIDE→ bodyThen ask:"Can you trace where the blood goes next?"The visual becomes part of the Board.==================================================25. MINI-LESSON DETOURIf the student lacks a prerequisite, create a temporary mini-lesson.Example:Main question:"Why does acceleration increase?"Student doesn't understand velocity.AI:### Quick detourBefore we continue, let's make velocity clear.[Mini explanation]Then:### Back to your questionNow, if velocity changes...Return to the original learning path.Track the prerequisite gap.==================================================26. ACTIVE RECALLAfter teaching an important concept, ask the student to recall it without looking.Example:AI explains:"Chlorophyll absorbs light energy."Later:"Without looking back, what does chlorophyll do?"This verifies understanding.==================================================27. EXPLAIN-BACKUse teach-back as a mastery test.AI:"Now explain photosynthesis to me as if you were teaching someone younger than you."The AI evaluates:- Accuracy- Completeness- Relationships- MisconceptionsThis can be a strong mastery signal.==================================================28. APPLICATION CHECKAfter conceptual learning, create a small variation.Example:Student understands:"Plants need sunlight."AI asks:"What do you predict would happen if a plant received no light?"This tests whether the student can apply the concept rather than repeat it.==================================================29. GENERALIZATIONWhere appropriate, test whether understanding transfers.Example:Student understands:"Heavy objects don't necessarily fall faster."AI:"Would the same reasoning apply on the Moon?"This is a deeper understanding check.==================================================30. DIFFICULTY CONTROLMaintain an internal difficulty level.Possible levels:1. Very Easy2. Easy3. Moderate4. Challenging5. Advanced6. ExpertAdapt based on:- accuracy- hints- time- mistakes- confidence- previous performanceDo not make difficulty increase simply because the student got one question right.==================================================31. STUCK DETECTIONDetect when the student may be stuck.Possible signals:- repeated incorrect attempts- repeated "I don't know"- long inactivity- repeated deletion/retyping- repeated requests for hints- circular reasoningIf stuck:Do not bombard the student with information.Offer:"Want a smaller clue?"[Smaller Clue][Show Example][Explain Visually][Explain It]==================================================32. INACTIVITYDo not immediately interpret inactivity as lack of understanding.The student may be thinking.Use reasonable delay before offering help.Example:"Take your time. If you want help, I'm here."Do not automatically reveal the answer.==================================================33. FRUSTRATION-AWARE TEACHINGIf the system detects repeated failure, simplify the task.Example:Original:"Explain why the reaction rate changes with temperature."Simplify:"First: what happens to particles when temperature increases?"Then:"What does that do to collisions?"Then return to the original question.The system should reduce cognitive load rather than simply repeat the original question.==================================================34. COGNITIVE LOAD CONTROLDo not put too many instructions on the Board at once.Prefer:ONE MAIN THOUGHT+ONE ACTION+OPTIONAL HINTExample:"First, identify what enters the plant."rather than:"Identify the reactants, explain their sources, describe the mechanism, write the equation and discuss the products."==================================================35. STUDENT CONTROLThe student must always have ways to control the level of help.Buttons:💡 Hint🧠 Analyze Board❓ Why?🎨 Explain Visually🔄 Try Another Explanation📚 Teach Me This⏭ ContinueThe AI guides, but the student remains in control.==================================================36. "TRY ANOTHER WAY"Provide:### 🔄 Explain Another WayIf the student doesn't understand.The AI should generate a genuinely different explanation.Do not merely rewrite the same explanation.Possible changes:Text → analogyText → diagramTheory → exampleEquation → visualDefinition → real-world situation==================================================37. "MAKE IT EASIER"Provide an option:### Make It EasierThe AI simplifies:- vocabulary- number of concepts- examples- question wordingIt should not change the actual learning objective.==================================================38. "GO DEEPER"For advanced learners:### Go DeeperThe AI can add:- mechanisms- exceptions- advanced terminology- applications- deeper connectionsThis keeps StepWise useful beyond basic education.==================================================39. ANALYZE BOARDWhen the student presses:🧠 Analyze Boardperform a complete teaching analysis.Evaluate:1. Current question2. Learning Blueprint3. Student-created objects4. AI-created objects5. Student corrections6. Concept understanding7. Missing concepts8. Misconceptions9. Current progress10. Next best actionCreate feedback directly on the Board.==================================================40. ANALYZE SELECTED CONTENTIf the student selects one object:Analyze ONLY that object plus necessary context.Example:Selected:"Plants get food from soil."AI:🔴 Concept ErrorThen provide the targeted explanation.Do not analyze the entire lesson unnecessarily.==================================================41. BOARD FEEDBACK LABELSUse consistent labels:🟢 CORRECT UNDERSTANDING🟡 PARTIALLY CORRECT🔵 MISSING IDEA🔴 CONCEPT ERROR🟣 THINK ABOUT THIS🟠 CHECK THIS STEP⚪ AI UNCERTAINThe exact colors and icons can be refined by the UI system, but semantic labels must always remain available.==================================================42. LABEL CONTENTEvery label should contain:- What was detected- Why it matters- What the student should think about- Optional hint- Optional actionExample:🔴 CONCEPT ERRORDetected:"You wrote that current is the same as voltage."Why:These are different electrical quantities.Think about:What represents the flow of charge?[Hint]==================================================43. CORRECTION TRACKINGFor every meaningful mistake, record:- Original student content- Error type- Concept- Severity- AI intervention- Hints provided- Student correction- Whether correction was successful- Time to correction- Whether student self-correctedThis data feeds the final learning report and long-term journey.==================================================44. ERROR TYPESClassify errors where possible:- Conceptual- Procedural- Calculation- Logical- Factual- Missing prerequisite- Misinterpretation- Incomplete- Language-related- Recognition uncertainty- Careless/minor errorDo not confuse recognition uncertainty with student error.==================================================45. ERROR RECOVERYAfter a mistake:Do not simply erase it.The student should be able to see:ORIGINAL↓WHY IT WAS AN ISSUE↓HINT↓CORRECTION↓UNDERSTANDINGMistakes become part of the learning journey.==================================================46. MASTERy CHECKBefore completing a topic, the Teaching Engine should determine whether the student has sufficient evidence of understanding.Possible final checks:- Explain in own words- Solve a variation- Identify an example- Identify a non-example- Connect concepts- Draw a process- Teach someone else- Answer a "why" questionUse the most appropriate check for the subject.==================================================47. FINAL UNDERSTANDINGWhen mastery evidence is sufficient:Display:🎉 TOPIC UNDERSTOODThen show:- What the student successfully demonstrated- Important concepts- Corrections made- Remaining minor gaps- Optional deeper learningDo not claim "perfect mastery" unless the evidence genuinely supports it.==================================================48. FINAL ANSWEROnly after the learning process should the AI provide the polished final explanation when appropriate.Structure:### Your UnderstandingThe student's own final explanation.### Refined ExplanationA clearer version preserving the student's meaning.### Complete AnswerA detailed academically appropriate answer.### Key IdeasImportant concepts.This allows the student to compare their thinking with the final understanding.==================================================49. SESSION REPORTGenerate:TIME SPENTCONCEPTS UNDERSTOODERRORSCORRECTIONSHINTS USEDSELF-CORRECTIONSVISUALS USEDPREREQUISITES DISCOVEREDINITIAL UNDERSTANDINGFINAL UNDERSTANDINGRECOMMENDED REVIEWRECOMMENDED NEXT TOPIC==================================================50. LEARNING JOURNEY UPDATEAfter completion, update the student's long-term learning map.For each concept:- mastery status- confidence- misconceptions resolved- remaining gaps- evidence- date learned- review recommendationExample:Biology→ Plant Biology→ Photosynthesis   ✓ Definition   ✓ Inputs   ✓ Products   🟡 Detailed mechanism   🔵 Chemical equation==================================================51. SPACED REVIEW SUPPORTWhere appropriate, recommend future review.Do not simply say:"Study this again."Instead identify the specific concept.Example:"Review the role of carbon dioxide in 3 days."Then later:"Can you explain why carbon dioxide is necessary?"Use active recall rather than simply showing the answer again.==================================================52. NO FALSE MASTERYNever mark a concept mastered because:- The student clicked continue.- The AI explained it.- The student copied the answer.- The student said "I understand."Require evidence where possible.==================================================53. TEACHING PERSONALIZATIONOver time, learn which teaching strategies appear effective for the student.For example:If the student consistently improves after diagrams:Use more visual explanations.If examples work better:Use examples.If the student prefers reasoning questions:Use guided questions.Do not make permanent assumptions from a single session.==================================================54. AGE-ADAPTIVE TEACHINGThe Teaching Engine must work across age groups.Young learner:"Let's figure this out together. What does the plant need from the Sun?"Older learner:"Let's establish the role of light energy in the reaction."Same concept.Different teaching presentation.==================================================55. SUBJECT-ADAPTIVE TEACHINGThe Teaching Engine must adapt by subject.Mathematics:Focus on procedural reasoning.Physics:Focus on models, relationships, units, assumptions and equations.Chemistry:Focus on particles, reactions, mechanisms and representations.Biology:Focus on structures, processes and relationships.Computer science:Focus on algorithms, logic and debugging.History:Focus on chronology, causation, evidence and interpretation.Literature:Focus on textual evidence, interpretation and argument.Languages:Focus on vocabulary, grammar, usage and communication.The engine must remain flexible enough for subjects not explicitly listed.==================================================56. DO NOT TURN EVERY LESSON INTO THE SAME TEMPLATEA physics numerical should not feel identical to a history question.A biology diagram should not feel identical to a mathematics proof.The Teaching Engine should adapt the learning interaction to the knowledge domain.==================================================57. SAFETY AGAINST OVER-INTERVENTIONNever constantly interrupt the student.The ideal feeling is:"The AI is there when I need it."not:"The AI keeps interrupting me."Use confidence, timing and context to determine intervention.==================================================58. SAFETY AGAINST UNDER-INTERVENTIONDo not allow major misconceptions to compound silently.If a student is confidently building an incorrect chain of reasoning:Pause.Highlight the problem.Explain what needs reconsideration.Give a targeted hint.Then let the student continue.==================================================59. TEACHING ENGINE DECISION MODELFor every meaningful student action, internally determine:STUDENT STATE:- confused- progressing- confident- stuck- correct- partially correct- incorrect- uncertainLEARNING STATE:- new concept- developing concept- prerequisite gap- integration- mastery checkINTERVENTION:- none- encouragement- question- hint- stronger hint- example- visual- mini-lesson- partial demonstration- full explanationNEXT ACTION:- continue- retry- clarify- revisit- advance- test- complete==================================================60. FINAL TEACHING ENGINE RULEThe Teaching Engine must always preserve the central StepWise philosophy:The AI should not ask:"How can I give this student the answer?"It should ask:"How can I help this student take the next step toward understanding?"Every hint should have a purpose.Every intervention should have a reason.Every correction should teach something.Every mistake should become useful information.Every successful correction should become evidence of growth.And every completed lesson should leave the student more capable of solving the next problem independently.
