STEPWISE AIPROMPT 02 — INTELLIGENT BOARD & CANVAS ENGINEBuild the StepWise AI learning Board as the primary workspace and central product experience.The Board must not feel like a simple drawing canvas.It is an AI-aware infinite learning canvas where the student thinks, writes, speaks, organizes information, makes mistakes, receives guidance, and builds understanding.The Board must work for theoretical questions, numerical problems, diagrams, explanations, essays, programming, science, mathematics, and open-ended learning.---1. BOARD OPENINGAfter a student submits a question, transition directly into the Board.Do not open a traditional chat interface first.The Board should appear with:- The original question- An AI introduction- The first learning prompt- A hint where appropriate- A progress indicator- The student's main workspace- Board tools- AI controlsExample:QuestionWhat is photosynthesis?Then AI places a learning card:«🌱 Let's explore photosynthesis.We'll build the answer together.Start by telling me what you think photosynthesis is.💡 Hint: Think about what plants do with sunlight.»The student can immediately begin working.---2. INFINITE CANVASThe Board should behave like an infinite canvas.Students can move around freely.Support:- Pan- Zoom in- Zoom out- Fit to content- Center on current activity- Reset viewThe canvas should not feel constrained to a single page.The student should be able to expand their thinking as much as necessary.---3. PRIMARY BOARD PRINCIPLEThe student should spend most of the learning session on the Board.Avoid unnecessary navigation away from the Board.AI explanations, hints, feedback, diagrams, concept cards, and progress should appear within the Board environment.The Board should feel like:«Notebook + whiteboard + visual mind map + AI teacher + knowledge workspace»combined into one experience.---4. STUDENT-CREATED OBJECTSThe student can create objects on the Board.Supported objects:TextTyped text blocks.HandwritingFreehand writing/strokes.Voice transcriptionSpeech converted into editable text objects.DrawingFreehand drawings.Shapes- Rectangle- Circle- Line- Arrow- Triangle- Basic geometric shapesSticky notesShort thoughts or reminders.ImagesUploaded or captured images.ConnectorsArrows and lines connecting ideas.Mathematical expressionsEquations and formulas.---5. MOVABLE TEXTEvery text object must be independently movable.Students can:- Drag- Drop- Resize- Rotate where appropriate- Duplicate- Delete- RepositionText should not automatically behave like a normal document where everything is locked into a paragraph structure.The student should be able to create a visual arrangement.Example:          Photosynthesis     Sunlight        CO₂         ↓             ↓              🌿               ↓          Glucose + O₂The spatial arrangement itself carries meaning.---6. TEXT EDITINGStudents should be able to edit their own text.Support:- Font size- Bold- Italic- Underline- Text alignment- Lists- Basic formatting- Mathematical symbols where appropriateKeep formatting simple and intuitive.Do not turn the Board into a complicated word processor.The purpose is thinking, not document formatting.---7. HANDWRITINGProvide a handwriting mode optimized for:- touch- stylus- mouseTools:- Pen- Pencil- Highlighter- Eraser- Lasso/select- Undo- RedoHandwriting should remain visually natural.The AI must be able to interpret handwriting as part of the student's thinking.Where appropriate, the system may recognize handwriting and associate it with structured meaning.---8. VOICE-TO-BOARDThe student can press:🎤 SpeakThen speak naturally.Example:«"I think photosynthesis happens because plants use sunlight to make food."»The system converts the speech into a text object on the Board.The student can then:- Move it- Edit it- Add to it- Connect it- Ask AI to analyze itVoice input should become part of the Board rather than simply appearing as a chat transcript.---9. OBJECT SPATIAL AWARENESSThe AI must understand the position of objects.For example:CO₂ ↓Leaf ↓GlucoseThe AI should recognize that the student may be expressing a relationship.Similarly:Mitosis     Meiosis   ↓           ↓2 cells      4 cellsThe AI should interpret this as a comparison structure.Spatial information should be included in the Board's internal representation.---10. CONNECTORSStudents can connect objects using:- Arrows- Lines- Curved connectorsThe AI should understand connector direction where possible.Example:Sunlight → Plantmay indicate:«sunlight affects/provides energy to the plant.»The AI should use these relationships when analyzing understanding.---11. AI-GENERATED OBJECTSThe AI can create Board objects when they improve learning.Examples:- Diagram- Flowchart- Concept map- Formula card- Explanation card- Example card- Comparison table- Timeline- Graph- Mind map- IllustrationThese objects must be visually polished and educationally useful.---12. AI OBJECTS ARE MOVABLEAI-generated objects can be:- Moved- Resized- Repositioned- Deleted- Duplicated- Annotated by the studentThey should behave like normal Board objects.---13. AI OBJECTS ARE NOT DIRECTLY EDITABLEThe student should not directly modify the internal content of an AI-generated educational object.For example, if AI generates a diagram showing:Sunlight → Plant → Glucosethe student cannot simply edit the AI's labels as though they created the diagram.However, they can:- Write on it- Circle it- Draw arrows- Add their own text- Add notes- Ask AI to modify/regenerate itThis preserves the distinction between AI-provided knowledge and student-generated reasoning.---14. STUDENT ANNOTATION ON AI OBJECTSThe student must be able to write over or around AI-generated objects.Example:AI generates a plant diagram.Student writes:«CO₂»and draws:«CO₂ → Leaf»The AI must treat that annotation as student reasoning.This allows the student to actively interact with AI-generated teaching material.---15. AI ANNOTATION OBJECTSWhen AI detects an issue, it should create an annotation.Examples:🔴 Concept Error🟡 Partially Correct🔵 Missing Idea🟣 Think About This🟢 Correct UnderstandingAnnotations can be attached to:- Text- Handwriting- Diagram regions- Equations- Objects- Connections---16. HIGHLIGHTINGAI should be able to highlight the exact area relevant to feedback.For example:Student writes:«Photosynthesis is when photos are made.»The phrase:photos are madeshould be highlighted.Do not highlight the entire Board unnecessarily.The student should immediately understand:«"This is the part I need to think about."»---17. LABEL + EXPLANATIONEvery important AI highlight should have an associated label.Example:🔴 Concept ErrorTap it.Display:«Why is this highlighted?This phrase suggests photographs, but photosynthesis is a biological process involving how plants produce food using light energy.💡 Think about what plants actually produce.»Actions:Try AgainGive Me Another HintExplain VisuallyWhy?---18. BOARD TOOLBARCreate a clean primary toolbar.Core tools:- Select- Text- Pen- Highlighter- Eraser- Shapes- Arrow- Image- Voice- Formula- Sticky note- Undo- RedoAvoid overwhelming young students.Advanced tools can be hidden under:More---19. AI TOOLBARCreate a separate AI control area.Important actions:🧠 Analyze BoardAnalyze everything currently on the canvas.💡 HintGive the next useful hint.👀 ExplainExplain the selected concept.🎨 VisualizeGenerate a visual explanation.🗺 Map ItCreate a concept map.🔗 ConnectSuggest relationships between selected objects.🧹 OrganizeClean and organize the Board.📝 SummarizeCreate a summary of the student's current understanding.❓ Ask AIAsk a question about the current Board.---20. SELECT-AND-ASKThe student should be able to select any Board object and ask AI about it.Example:Student selects:«"Mitochondria produces energy."»Then chooses:AnalyzeAI responds:«Your main idea is correct, but "energy" is a little too broad. Mitochondria are involved in producing ATP, which cells use as an energy currency.»The AI should analyze the selected content in context.---21. BOARD ANALYSISWhen the student presses:Analyze Boardthe AI should inspect the entire canvas.It should consider:- All student text- Handwriting- Voice transcription- Diagrams- Drawings- Connections- Spatial relationships- Corrections- Previous AI feedback- Current question- Learning BlueprintThen generate appropriate feedback.---22. BOARD ANALYSIS SHOULD NOT DESTROY THE BOARDAnalyzing the Board must not automatically rearrange or overwrite the student's work.AI annotations should be additional objects.The original student work must remain intact.The student can dismiss AI annotations.---23. ORGANIZE BOARDProvide:Organize BoardAI can propose a cleaner arrangement.Before making significant changes, either:- create a preview, or- allow immediate action with Undo.Never destroy the student's original arrangement.Organization should preserve meaning and relationships.---24. BOARD VERSION HISTORYSave meaningful Board states.For example:Initial attemptAfter first correctionAfter visual explanationFinal understandingAllow the student to return to previous states when technically practical.---25. UNDO EVERYTHINGEvery destructive or major Board operation should support Undo.This includes:- Delete- Move where appropriate- Organize- AI-generated changes- Object replacementNever make the student afraid to experiment.---26. SAVE SYSTEMBoards should automatically save.Use autosave.Display a subtle status:SavedorSaving...The student should never have to worry about losing their work.If there is a network interruption, preserve local changes where technically possible and synchronize when the connection returns.---27. BOARD LIBRARYStudents should have access to:My BoardsOrganize Boards by:- Recent- Subject- Topic- Favorites- ArchivedEach Board should show:- Name- Topic- Last edited- Progress/completion status- Thumbnail---28. BOARD NAMINGAutomatically suggest names based on the question.Example:Question:«What is photosynthesis?»Suggested Board name:Photosynthesis — Understanding the ProcessStudent can rename it.---29. EXPORTProvide:Save as ImageExport the current Board as a high-quality image.Support:- PNG- JPGThe export should include the visible Board content and maintain readable layout.Provide a clean export mode that can optionally hide temporary UI controls.---30. SHAREArchitect the system so sharing can be added.Possible future options:- Share image- Share read-only Board- Share with teacher- Share with classmatesDo not make sharing public by default.---31. CHILD-FRIENDLY BOARDThe Board must work for young children.For younger users:- Larger touch targets- Simpler toolbar- More visual controls- Fewer advanced options- Simple AI wording- Friendly iconsFor older users:- More advanced mathematical tools- More formatting- More technical terminology- More complex diagramsThe same core Board remains underneath.---32. RESPONSIVE BOARDThe Board must work on:- Mobile phones- Tablets- Laptops- Desktop computersOn mobile:- Optimize for touch- Use floating toolbars- Avoid tiny controlsOn tablets:- Prioritize handwriting and landscape modeOn desktop:- Provide keyboard shortcuts- Support mouse interaction- Allow larger canvas navigation---33. ACCESSIBILITYSupport:- Keyboard navigation- Screen readers where possible- High contrast- Adjustable text size- Touch-friendly controls- Voice input- Clear labels- Non-color-dependent feedbackDo not communicate meaning through color alone.For example, a red error must also have:Concept Errortext label and/or icon.---34. BOARD PERFORMANCEThe Board must remain responsive even when it contains many objects.Use appropriate:- Canvas optimization- Object rendering optimization- Lazy loading- Efficient state management- Incremental AI analysis- Debounced input processingDo not send the entire Board to the AI after every tiny movement or pen stroke.---35. AI SHOULD KNOW WHEN TO INTERVENEThe Board must not constantly interrupt the student.AI should distinguish between:Student is still writingDo nothing.Student has completed a meaningful thoughtAnalyze it.Clear high-confidence errorIntervene.Partial understandingAsk or hint.AI uncertainAsk for clarification.This prevents the Board from feeling intrusive.---36. BOARD STATEMaintain a structured representation of:- Objects- Object types- Object positions- Object sizes- Object relationships- Student-created vs AI-created- Timestamps- Associated concepts- AI annotations- Current question- Current learning stage- Corrections- Learning historyThis structured state is essential for the AI reasoning engine.---37. BOARD SAFETYEvery student's Board is private by default.Implement proper:- Authentication- Authorization- Data isolation- Secure storage- Access controlA student must not accidentally see another student's Board.---38. BOARD AS KNOWLEDGEThe Board should ultimately represent the student's evolving understanding.At the beginning:Low understandingDuring the session:Ideas + mistakes + hints + correctionsAt the end:Structured understandingThe final Board should feel like something the student created and can use later for revision.---39. FINAL BOARD EXPERIENCEAt completion, the Board should contain:- Original question- Student's reasoning- Important corrections- Useful AI visuals- Key concepts- Final understanding- Concept relationships- Optional final summaryThe student should be able to look at the Board and understand the topic without reopening the AI conversation.---40. NON-NEGOTIABLE BOARD RULEDo not build StepWise as:Chat interface + whiteboard feature.Build it as:«An intelligent learning canvas with AI embedded into every part of the learning experience.»The Board is where the student:thinks, writes, speaks, makes mistakes, receives guidance, visualizes ideas, connects concepts, corrects themselves, and ultimately demonstrates understanding.Everything else in StepWise AI must support this Board-first experience.
