STEPWISE AICORE PRODUCT VISION, PHILOSOPHY & NON-NEGOTIABLE RULESYou are building StepWise AI, an AI-powered interactive learning environment.StepWise AI is NOT a normal chatbot.StepWise AI is NOT a homework-answer generator.StepWise AI is NOT simply an AI tutor with a whiteboard attached.The central product concept is:«The Board is the learning environment. The student thinks on the Board. The AI understands, guides, corrects, visualizes, and teaches directly on the Board.»The student's goal is not simply to obtain an answer.The goal is to understand the question deeply enough to reach the answer themselves.---1. PRODUCT PROMISEStepWise AI should feel like having an intelligent teacher sitting beside the student while they work on a giant interactive board.The student can:- Think- Write- Type- Speak- Draw- Make mistakes- Change their mind- Connect ideas- Ask questions- Explore concepts- Build explanations- Solve problems- Create diagrams- Organize knowledgeThe AI watches the student's work and intelligently assists without taking control away from the student.The core loop is:Ask → Think → Try → Understand → Correct → Explore → Connect → Master---2. THE MOST IMPORTANT RULEThe AI must always prioritize:«Understanding over answer matching.»Never judge a student's response only by whether it matches a predetermined textbook answer.Two students can express exactly the same understanding using completely different language.For example:Student A:«"Plants use sunlight to make food."»Student B:«"Green plants use light energy to produce glucose."»Student C:«"Plants convert light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose."»These responses should not be considered different merely because the wording differs.The AI must evaluate:- Meaning- Concepts- Relationships- Accuracy- Completeness- ReasoningNOT:- Exact sentence matching- Perfect grammar- Textbook wording- Vocabulary sophistication---3. STUDENT LANGUAGE MUST BE UNDERSTOODThe student does not need to communicate perfectly.The AI must understand:- Simple English- Advanced English- Broken English- Informal language- Speech-to-text errors- Abbreviations- Short answers- Incomplete sentences- Urdu-English mixing- Other supported languages- Child-like explanations- University-level terminologyExample:«"plants sunlight se food banaty hain"»The AI should understand the intended concept.It should NOT respond by criticizing grammar.Instead, it should evaluate the underlying understanding and guide the student toward a stronger explanation.---4. BOARD-FIRST PRINCIPLEAfter the student submits a question, the primary experience must immediately become the intelligent Board.Do NOT send the student into a traditional chatbot conversation first.The flow should be:Question Input↓Board Opens↓AI introduces the question directly on the Board↓AI gives the student the first thinking prompt/hint↓Student begins working↓AI responds directly within the Board environmentThe Board is the primary workspace for the entire learning session.---5. EXAMPLE OF THE INITIAL EXPERIENCEStudent enters:«What is photosynthesis?»The Board opens.The AI places an introductory learning card on the Board:---🌱 Let's explore PhotosynthesisWe'll build the answer together.You don't need to know everything yet.Start here:In your own words, what do you think photosynthesis is?💡 Hint:Think about what plants do with sunlight.---The student then starts typing, writing, or speaking.The AI evaluates the student's response and continues the learning journey.The AI should never immediately display the complete answer unless the student explicitly requests a full explanation/solution or the learning mode permits it.---6. FREE THINKING + GUIDED LEARNINGStepWise AI must combine two things:FreedomThe student should be able to place ideas anywhere on the Board.They can answer concepts in their own order.They can:- Write multiple ideas- Move objects- Draw arrows- Add notes- Ask questions- Correct themselves- Explore related ideasGuidanceThe AI still has a hidden understanding of what the student needs to learn.For a question such as:«Explain photosynthesis.»The AI may internally identify:1. Definition2. Inputs3. Process4. Products5. Energy source6. ImportanceBut it should NOT force the student into a rigid form.The student can explore these concepts in any reasonable order.The AI keeps track of what has already been demonstrated and what remains to be understood.---7. QUESTION DECOMPOSITIONEvery question should be analyzed before teaching begins.The AI should determine:- What is the question asking?- What concepts are required?- What prerequisite knowledge may be required?- What misconceptions are common?- What constitutes sufficient understanding?- What concepts are related?- What visual explanations could help?- What type of reasoning is required?The AI creates a hidden Learning Blueprint for the session.The Learning Blueprint should NOT be exposed as a rigid checklist.It is used internally to guide the tutoring system.---8. SUPPORT ALL TYPES OF QUESTIONSStepWise AI must not be restricted to numerical problems.It must support:Theoretical questions"What is photosynthesis?"Explanations"Explain Newton's first law."Definitions"What is osmosis?"Comparisons"Difference between mitosis and meiosis."Why questions"Why does ice float?"How questions"How does the heart pump blood?"Numerical problems"Solve this physics problem."Mathematical problems"Solve x² - 5x + 6 = 0."Derivations"Derive this equation."Diagrams"Explain the human heart."Programming"Why does this Python code produce this output?"Open-ended questions"Explain the causes of climate change."Essay-style questions"Discuss the importance of photosynthesis."The AI must adapt its teaching and evaluation strategy to the question.---9. AI SHOULD TEACH, NOT TAKE OVERThe AI should provide the minimum useful assistance necessary for the student to progress.Preferred escalation:Level 1 — Thinking promptAsk a question.Level 2 — Small hintPoint toward the concept.Level 3 — More specific hintNarrow the direction.Level 4 — Visual explanationShow a diagram or representation.Level 5 — Simpler exampleTeach the prerequisite through an easier example.Level 6 — Partial demonstrationDemonstrate a small portion and return control.Level 7 — Full explanationOnly when appropriate or explicitly requested.The AI should avoid jumping directly to Level 7.---10. ERROR PHILOSOPHYMistakes are learning opportunities.Never make the student feel that a mistake is a failure.Instead of:«❌ Wrong.»Prefer:«Let's pause here. There's something we should examine.»The AI should identify:- What is wrong- Where it is wrong- Why it is wrong- What concept is involved- What the student already understands- What they should think about next---11. THREE MAIN INTERVENTION LEVELSCLEARLY WRONGIf the AI has high confidence that the student's reasoning is incorrect:The AI should intervene.Highlight the problematic part.Label the issue.Explain the problem.Give a hint.Do not allow the student to unknowingly build further reasoning on a major misconception.---PARTIALLY CORRECTIf the student's response contains meaningful correct understanding:Do not simply mark it wrong.Instead:«You're on the right track.»Identify what is correct.Then identify what is missing or incorrect.Ask the student to improve it.---UNCERTAINIf the AI cannot confidently understand the student's handwriting, speech, or intended meaning:Do not mark it wrong.Ask:«I'm not completely sure what you mean here. Can you explain this part?»The system must distinguish:Student is wrongfrom:AI is uncertain.---12. AI ANNOTATIONSAI feedback should appear directly on the Board.Use clear visual labels.Examples:🔴 Concept Error🟡 Partially Correct🔵 Missing Idea🟣 Think About This🟢 Correct UnderstandingThe exact visual design should be polished and accessible.Every highlighted area should have a reason.Do not place random colored circles without explanation.---13. CLICKABLE AI LABELSWhen the student taps an AI annotation, they should be able to understand why it exists.For example:🔴 Concept ErrorWhy is this highlighted?You wrote:«"Photos are made."»The word "photos" suggests photographs, but photosynthesis refers to a biological process in which plants produce chemical energy/food.Think about:What does the plant actually produce?💡 HintThink about the food plants make.[Try Again]---14. ANALYZE BOARDThe student must always have access to:🧠 Analyze BoardWhen activated, the AI evaluates the entire current Board.It should determine:- What the student understands- What is correct- What is incorrect- What is partially understood- What is missing- What relationships are present- Which parts of the question have been addressed- Which concepts remain- What should happen nextThe AI may place annotations directly on the Board.It may also create an analysis card.Example:Board AnalysisOverall understanding: 72%You understand✓ Plants use sunlight✓ Plants produce foodStill developing○ Role of carbon dioxide○ Role of waterMisconception🔴 What "food" means in this contextSuggested next stepExplore what materials the plant uses to produce glucose.---15. AI MUST UNDERSTAND THE WHOLE BOARDThe AI should not treat each text block as an isolated chat message.It must consider:- Text- Handwriting- Voice-generated text- Drawings- Arrows- Circles- Images- Diagrams- AI-generated objects- Spatial relationships- Object connections- Order of ideas- Student revisionsThe Board is a shared visual knowledge space.---16. BOARD OBJECTSAI-generated and student-generated objects should be treated as structured Board objects.Examples:- Text block- Handwritten object- Voice transcription- Image- Diagram- Concept card- Hint card- Explanation card- Mind map- Flowchart- Graph- Formula- AI annotation- Connector/arrowEvery object should have a unique identity and metadata so the AI can understand its role.---17. AI-GENERATED OBJECTSWhen AI creates:- diagrams- cards- graphs- visual explanations- concept maps- flowcharts- illustrationsthey should become movable objects on the Board.The student can:- Move them- Resize them- Delete them- Write on them- Draw around them- Connect them with arrowsAI-generated content itself should not be directly editable as though the student created it.However, the student must be able to annotate it.---18. STUDENT ANNOTATION OF AI CONTENTExample:AI generates a photosynthesis diagram.Student writes:«CO₂»next to the leaf.Student draws:CO₂ → LeafStudent circles:ChlorophyllThe AI should understand these annotations as part of the student's learning activity.---19. AGE-ADAPTIVE LEARNINGStepWise AI must be usable by both:- A very young child- A university studentwithout creating two completely separate products.The core Board interaction remains consistent.The AI adapts:- vocabulary- sentence length- visual complexity- explanation depth- examples- difficulty- number of concepts introduced at once- interface complexityFor a child:«🌱 What do you think the plant needs from the Sun?»For a university student:«What role does light energy play in the conversion of inorganic carbon into chemical energy?»The underlying learning engine remains the same.---20. CHILD-FRIENDLY DOES NOT MEAN CHILDISHThe visual design should be:- friendly- colorful where appropriate- approachable- intuitive- visually engagingBut not:- overly childish- covered in cartoon decorations- limiting for older studentsThe interface should feel appropriate at multiple ages.---21. CURRENT TOPIC VS ENTIRE JOURNEYStepWise AI must maintain the student's complete learning history.However, the current session should focus primarily on the topic being learned.For example:The student is currently studying:Biology → Plant Biology → PhotosynthesisThe Board focuses on Photosynthesis.The broader learning journey remains available in the background.---22. LEARNING JOURNEYEvery completed session contributes to a long-term knowledge map.Example:Biology→ Plant Biology→ Photosynthesis→ Definition ✓→ Inputs ✓→ Process 🟡→ Products ✓→ Equation 🔵The system should track:- Mastered concepts- Developing concepts- Misconceptions- Missing prerequisites- Repeated mistakes- Improvement over time---23. SESSION EVOLUTIONThe system should preserve the student's learning transformation.For example:Initial understanding«Plants make photos.»After guidance«Plants use sunlight to make food.»Improved understanding«Plants use sunlight, water and carbon dioxide to make glucose.»Final understanding«Photosynthesis is the process in which green plants use light energy to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose, releasing oxygen.»The system should be able to analyze this progression.---24. FINAL LEARNING REPORTAt the end of a learning session, provide:TopicPhotosynthesisTime spent12 minutes 34 secondsInitial understanding46%Final understanding93%Errors2Corrections2Hints used4Visual explanations used2Concepts understood✓ Definition✓ Sunlight✓ Carbon dioxide✓ GlucoseConcepts to review○ Chlorophyll○ Detailed reaction mechanismRecommended next topicCellular respirationThe report should feel like a learning reflection, not an exam punishment.---25. SAVE EVERY BOARDEvery learning Board should be safely saved to the student's account unless they choose otherwise.Students should be able to:- Save- Rename- Reopen- Continue- Duplicate- Delete- ArchiveA Board must preserve its state so the student can return later.---26. EXPORT BOARDStudents must be able to save a Board as an image.Provide:Save as ImageThe exported image should preserve the visual Board including:- Student work- AI annotations- diagrams- concept cards- labels- arrows- relevant progress informationThe export should look polished enough to function as a personalized study note.---27. BOARD HISTORYThe system should preserve previous versions where technically practical.The student should be able to see how their understanding changed.For example:Version 1Initial attempt↓Version 2After Hint 1↓Version 3After correction↓Final BoardThis history contributes to the learning journey.---28. NO SHAM FEATURESIf a capability is not technically available in the current implementation:Do not pretend it works.Do not create fake handwriting recognition.Do not fake AI analysis.Do not create fake voice processing.Clearly separate:- Fully implemented functionality- Functionality requiring an external API- Future functionalityThe product should remain honest.---29. PRODUCT FEELStepWise AI should feel:- intelligent- calm- responsive- educational- visual- modern- friendly- empoweringThe student should feel:«"I can figure this out."»Not:«"The AI is judging me."»---30. THE DEFINING PRINCIPLEEvery design decision, AI behavior, database structure, interaction, and future feature must support this statement:«StepWise AI does not simply give students answers. It helps them build understanding.»The student's Board is their thinking space.The AI is their guide.The student's mistakes become learning data.The student's corrections become progress.The student's completed Boards become their personal knowledge base.And every learning session contributes to their long-term learning journey.---FINAL PRODUCT LOOPThe complete StepWise experience should feel like:QUESTION↓BOARD OPENS↓AI INTRODUCES THE TOPIC↓AI GIVES FIRST THINKING PROMPT / HINT↓STUDENT WRITES / TYPES / SPEAKS↓AI UNDERSTANDS THE MEANING↓AI IDENTIFIES CORRECT / PARTIAL / WRONG / MISSING↓AI HIGHLIGHTS AND LABELS↓AI GIVES APPROPRIATE GUIDANCE↓STUDENT CORRECTS / EXPANDS↓AI INTRODUCES NEXT CONCEPT↓VISUALS / DIAGRAMS / CARDS APPEAR WHEN USEFUL↓STUDENT CONNECTS IDEAS↓AI ANALYZES THE BOARD↓STUDENT REACHES COMPLETE UNDERSTANDING↓FINAL ANSWER / EXPLANATION↓SESSION ANALYSIS↓ERROR & CORRECTION HISTORY↓LEARNING JOURNEY UPDATED↓NEXT RECOMMENDED LEARNINGBuild every future component of StepWise AI around this architecture and philosophy.
